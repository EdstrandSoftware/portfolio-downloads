CSV Relay Lab — text alternative to the silent 156-second film

The film is an illustrated explanation, with one typeset excerpt from verified
saved-run results. Its diagrams and moving cards are not a screen recording of
live transfer activity. All services shown are simulated local HTTP services.

1. [00:00–00:12] Final outcome preview. Eight fictional CSV batches have arrived at the
local receiver. The film then rewinds to explain how this outcome was reached.

2. [00:12–00:26] Inputs and bytes. Eight files, parts-001 through parts-008, each contain
three fictional parts rows, for 24 rows total. Selected examples show mixed
line endings, a UTF-8 byte-order mark, Unicode and CSV quoting. Sent and
received byte examples match. The relay does not clean or interpret values.

3. [00:26–00:38] Setup. From api-transfer/toolproject/, the example command is
`python3 scripts/run_demo.py --output runs/my-demo-run`. Python 3.10+ and no
further package installation are required for the relay. One helper starts
two temporary local servers, runs the example, saves results in the chosen
fresh folder and stops the servers. The ZIP root WORKFLOW.md has setup steps.

4. [00:38–00:50] Dry run. Eight batches are checked. Nothing is sent, and no receiver
receipt or sender ledger is created.

5. [00:50–01:06] Deliberate faults. On the first transfer phase, six batches deliver and
two fail after the relevant bounded retry behavior. One 503 temporary error
is retried but still fails; a 400 bad request stops without retry. The six
completed deliveries remain recorded.

6. [01:06–01:30] Lost reply for parts-005. This batch is one of the six initial successes.
The local receiver stores its bytes, transfer key and receipt, but the first
reply is lost. The sender retries under the same key. The receiver returns
its original receipt: two POST requests, one stored receipt. This depends on
the receiver's atomic key/bytes/receipt behavior.

7. [01:30–01:40] Repair. The scripted helper automatically removes both faults it
injected. For a real failure, a person would first investigate and fix the
cause. This exercise does not repair production services.

8. [01:40–01:54] Recovery. A failed-only run delivers the two failed batches and skips six
completed ones. The sender ledger tracks progress; receiver receipts confirm
stored transfers. A normal rerun skips all eight and sends zero new POSTs.
`demo-summary.json` shows counts and `integrity-results.json` shows byte
comparisons in the selected run folder.

9. [01:54–02:10] New ninth batch. In a separate local scenario, a new file parts-009 arrives
under a new batch ID. Failed-only mode skips it because it has not failed.
Normal mode sends it, yielding nine total receipts while the first eight
receipt IDs stay unchanged. The next normal rerun sends zero new POSTs.

10. [02:10–02:22] Receiver contract. The mock receiver stores the transfer key, bytes and
receipt together. A typeset saved-run excerpt accompanies the diagram. A
sender ledger alone cannot prevent duplication after a reply is lost. This is
a contract demonstrated by this local receiver, not a general production
exactly-once guarantee.

root and use the runnable example in api-transfer/toolproject/. The film ends
on this package navigation cue.
